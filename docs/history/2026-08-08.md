### 2026-08-08 — The unnamed interjections get a list of their own

- Added: a fifth sidebar section, `Bez jména`, between `Mluvčí` and `Kontrola`.
  It lists the blocks recognition could not place — the short "Yeah.", "Děkuju."
  — and each row credits one with a single click.
- Clicking the row **plays** from its own moment rather than moving the cursor
  quietly. A third of a second of text says nothing about whose voice it is;
  the only way to answer the question the row asks is to hear it. Same rule as
  a note's time chip, and the opposite of `goTo`, which stays quiet because
  stepping through uncertain spots with F3 is reading.
- Changed with it, and this is the larger half: `Kontrola` and `Opravy` play
  too, and all three rows carry the sample button's own play triangle before
  the time. A play mark on a row that only seeks would lie; and an uncertain
  spot is verified by listening, so the change is right on its own terms.
- Only the two neighbouring voices are offered, not every speaker. A gap
  between two blocks of one person is already filled by `bridge_unknown` in the
  backend, so what is left lies between two *different* people and the answer
  is one of those two. Five voices under thirty rows would have been 150
  buttons of which 148 cannot be right. A third voice is still reachable from
  the transcript's own context menu.
- The section appears only when it has something in it, and only once somebody
  is recognised: before that every block is unnamed and the list says nothing.
- Files: `src/Detail.tsx`, `src/styles.css`, `src/icons.tsx`,
  `src/locales/{cs,en}/detail.ts`.
- Not verified in this session: the sandbox that runs commands ran out of disk
  before the gates could be run. `tsc`, `i18n:check` and the browser harness
  are all owed. One defect this cost is recorded in the entry below.

### 2026-08-08 — Names before the run, offered after it

- Added: a second step to the speaker-count dialog. Having said how many people
  speak, you can name them; the fields are optional and `Nevím` skips the step
  entirely, since not knowing the count means not knowing how many fields to
  draw.
- **What the names cannot do, stated because it is the whole design.** The
  clustering produces anonymous groups and nothing in them points at Roman, so
  names typed in advance cannot label anybody. What they are is a shortlist:
  after the run, clicking into a speaker's name field offers them as chips, one
  click each once the sample has been heard. The count remains the valuable
  half — 99.8/0.2 against seven groups for two people, measured.
- The chips appear under the row being named and never under all of them: five
  voices and five names would be twenty-five buttons where one is the answer.
  `onMouseDown` is prevented so the field keeps focus and the chips are still
  mounted when the click lands.
- `src/speakerNames.ts` keeps the list in local storage, not the archive. It is
  a shortlist, not data: the moment a name is on a voice the database holds it
  and this record has done its job. Losing it costs one round of typing — the
  same list the reader would have typed anyway. A column would have meant Rust,
  which could not be compiled or tested in this session, and two entries today
  are about what unverified code costs.
- Files: `src/SpeakerCountDialog.tsx`, `src/speakerNames.ts` (new),
  `src/App.tsx`, `src/Detail.tsx`, `src/styles.css`,
  `src/locales/{cs,en}/dialogs.ts`.

### 2026-08-08 — The sidebar is five cards, and the audit that got it there

- Changed: each sidebar section is a card that opens when its header is
  clicked, and the section's own action (`Rozpoznat mluvčí`, `Přidat`) moved
  **inside** the body, bottom right. That last move is what made the rest
  possible: with nothing competing on the header row, the whole width is one
  button, and every arrangement before it — a growing heading, a wrapping
  header, a second chevron button hidden from assistive technology — was a way
  around a button that may not contain another button.
- Four rounds of this were wrong, and a read-only audit against the file
  settled them rather than another guess:
  - The lift was written for `:not(.open)` while sections default to open, so
    it never fired. `.volba.zvolena` and `.radek.bezi` both keep their lift; a
    state that suppressed it would make this the only such surface in the app.
  - The hover was `hsl(var(--ds-foreground) / 0.05)`, invented on the spot,
    against thirteen rules using a named token. It is `--pozadi` now, the
    transcript's own ground, in the header and in the rows alike. `--tlumene`
    was tried and is one step too dark on `--panel`; it stays for the row that
    is sounding.
  - The radius was `--r-karta`. In this codebase `--r-velky` is the radius of a
    bordered card on `--panel` without exception, and `--r-karta` is what goes
    *inside* one. `overflow: hidden` went with it: the focus ring is an outer
    shadow and the toggle is flush with three edges.
  - Removing the icon's circle was wrong twice. The header kept its height and
    lost its object, so five text lines behind hairlines read as one grey slab
    — and `Library.tsx` reuses that rule for the archive's folder heading, so a
    second screen changed unintentionally. It is back at 30/17, the reduced
    variant the About rows already use *because* it is this panel's geometry.
- The card owns the gap under its heading. Left to themselves the five bodies
  began at 2, 2, 4 and 12 px, because each list carried a top margin of its own.
- Files: `src/Detail.tsx`, `src/styles.css`.

### 2026-08-08 — One rule for choosing among a row

- **The chosen one is filled with the opposite of its ground.** On the grey
  Settings page that is a white pill with `--stin`; on the dialog's white it is
  the dark fill the playback speeds already use. One rule, two expressions,
  and the difference is the ground rather than an exception.
- Supersedes the three-way split written earlier the same day — bordered pill
  for an action, dark pill for "a value that has been set", white pill for
  "navigation". The middle category was invented: a playback speed and a
  document tab are the same act, one of a row, chosen. That they looked
  different was not a rule but a drift.
- What survives: **a border means a standalone action** (`Uložit`, `Vylepšený
  přepis`) and nothing else. A choice among a row never has one.
- Applied to the improved-transcript preview, which had three heights in one
  strip. Its two rows are now split by what they belong to rather than by
  looks: `Přepis · Shrnutí · Překlad` chooses *which document* and stays on the
  dialog's white with the heading; version, summary length and target language
  are properties *of the document being read* and sit on the grey with it,
  carrying the edge between the two.
- Removed: the visible `Délka` and `Přeložit do` labels. The tab above says
  which view it is and the control below shows the value. The group keeps its
  `aria-label` and `Select` its `description`, so nothing was lost to a screen
  reader. Four keys and `.ai-output-label` went with them.
- `.segmented-control` now has exactly one user left, the archive's density
  toggle. That is an icon toggle rather than a row of tabs, so it stands on its
  own — but it is no longer a family, and the next person to reach for it
  should know that.
- Files: `src/Detail.tsx`, `src/styles.css`,
  `src/locales/{cs,en}/detail.ts`.

### 2026-08-08 — The archive header cannot make itself reopen

- What the user saw, in a small window: the collapsing hero flickered until the
  wheel had been turned a long way.
- Cause, and it is the one an earlier entry claimed was impossible. Collapsing
  takes ~267 px out of the page. In a low window with a short list the content
  then fits, so the browser must clamp `scrollTop` to zero — and zero after a
  positive position is exactly the test for a genuine return to the top. The
  header collapsed, reopened itself, grew tall enough to scroll again, and
  repeated. The comment saying the two conditions cannot chase each other was
  written about a resize, and missed the collapse itself.
- Fixed: for 250 ms after our own change, scroll events record where they are
  and decide nothing. A real gesture cannot cross the threshold and come back
  inside that window; a layout settling can only happen inside it.
- Fixed with it: collapsing by wheel sets `scrollTop = 0` and now tells the
  remembered position so too. It did not, so the next event looked like a fall
  from 300 to zero — a second way to read a layout consequence as an
  instruction.
- Files: `src/Library.tsx`.

### 2026-08-08 — Correcting entry: NSIS wants UTF-8 with no BOM, and Tauri adds it

- Two builds were lost to guessing, and sixteen bytes ended it. The first
  attempt failed with `Invalid command: "<BOM>;"`, the second — as UTF-16LE —
  with `Bad text encoding`.
- Measured rather than reasoned. The first bytes of the *copied* files:
  - `English.nsh`: `EF BB BF` then `LangString`
  - `Czech.nsh`: `EF BB BF` then `FF FE` then `; C z e` in UTF-16LE
- So **Tauri does not copy the language file, it reads it and writes it out
  with a UTF-8 BOM of its own.** A source carrying one ends up with two; a
  UTF-16 source ends up with a UTF-8 BOM in front of a UTF-16 one. The source
  must be UTF-8 with **no** BOM.
- Supersedes the claim in the entry that added `Czech.nsh`, which reasoned from
  the right observation to the wrong conclusion: `English.nsh` begins with a
  BOM because Tauri put it there, not because its source has one — and that
  file is pure ASCII, so it would have worked either way.
- `.gitattributes` returns `*.nsh` to `text eol=crlf`; the `binary` line added
  for the UTF-16 attempt is gone.
- Lesson, and it is this file's oldest one in new clothes: a diagnosis that
  fits something already written in the repository is the most persuasive kind
  and the easiest to get wrong. Sixteen bytes cost thirty seconds and settled
  what forty minutes of rebuilding could not.
- Files: `.gitattributes`, `src-tauri/nsis/Czech.nsh` (re-encoded).

### 2026-08-08 — The installer's bitmaps are supplied at 2×

- What the user saw: badly pixelated artwork in the installer.
- Cause: the artwork arrived at 2× and was resized down to the slots' own
  164×314 and 150×57, throwing the headroom away. `installer.nsi` sets
  `ManifestDPIAware true` and `PerMonitorV2` and defines no `NOSTRETCH`, so
  NSIS lays the page out in scaled pixels and stretches the bitmaps into it —
  at 150 % the slots are 246×471 and 225×85, at 200 % exactly twice the
  nominal size.
- Changed: **328×628 and 300×114**, 24-bit, which is pixel-for-pixel at 200 %
  and a clean downsample below it. Not larger: BMP is uncompressed, so 3× would
  add about 900 kB to the setup file for a difference beyond the DPI anyone
  runs an installer at.
- Confirmed by the build: speaker recognition works from the installed copy, so
  Tauri does bundle `onnxruntime.dll` and `DirectML.dll` from the target
  directory and no `resources` entry is needed. Still worth one look in
  `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Slobot` — if those two are not there, it is working from a
  copy that happens to be elsewhere on that one machine.
- Files: `src-tauri/nsis/sidebar.bmp`, `src-tauri/nsis/header.bmp`.
