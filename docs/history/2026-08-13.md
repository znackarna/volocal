### 2026-08-13 — Two clicks on *Zálohovat teď* in one second reported a broken archive

Found by pressing the button quickly in the running application:

```
output file already exists: Error code 1: SQL error or missing database
```

Nothing was broken. The stamp in a backup's name is accurate to the second —
`volocal-2026-08-13-004512.db` — and the button re-enables the moment the first
copy is written, so a second click inside the same second aims at a name that is
already taken. `VACUUM INTO` refuses to overwrite, and its refusal arrived on
screen wearing the words *SQL error or missing database*, which is the last
sentence anybody should read about an archive that is perfectly fine.

Fixed where the name is made, not where the copy is written: if a file for this
second is already there, it is the copy this call would have made, and it is
returned. What was asked for is a backup of now, and there is one.

Two fixes deliberately not made:

- **Widening the stamp.** `backup_stamp` parses exactly `0000-00-00-000000`,
  `list_backups` orders by that text, and the rotation reads the day out of it.
  Every backup already written on every machine is in that shape.
- **Overwriting.** `back_up`'s own comment says it refuses to clobber so that a
  timestamped name can never land on an older copy. That is worth keeping, and
  this case is the one time the name is not an older copy's.

- Files: `src-tauri/src/db.rs`.

the newest copy whatever the time — passes the first test and quietly stops

### 2026-08-13 — Words that share a timestamp stop sharing a moment

Whisper aligns tokens, not words, and a run of short tokens can decode to one
frame. Every word in such a run carried the run's time, so all of them seeked to
the first, all of them lit up together while the audio ran, and the context menu
offered one moment for each.

**Measured before anything was written**, over the archive as it stands: 812 of
8393 words — 9.7 % — sit in one of 335 such groups, of two to six words. That
matches the 11 % `CLAUDE.md` recorded for Janka. The cost of a click on the last
word of a group has a median of 0.13 s, which nobody can hear. The tail is the
reason this was worth doing: *A to téma je věrnost,* at 3:36 is five words over
3.14 s, so the last of them began two and a half seconds early.

**The estimate is checked, not asserted.** The words that share a time have no
ground truth. The ones that do not are the control: take three consecutive words
with three measured times, hide the middle, predict it the way a tied word is
now predicted, and compare. Over 6905 such triples:

| | mean | median | 90th | worst |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| what it did before | 0.340 s | 0.260 s | 0.680 s | 3.930 s |
| the estimate | 0.101 s | 0.080 s | 0.204 s | 1.160 s |

Closer in 92.2 % of cases, further in 2.2 %.

**It buys that with a failure the old behaviour could not have.** Sitting on the
group's start was always at or before the word; an estimate can land after it
and clip the word that was clicked. That happens in 15.4 % of the control, by a
median of 0.048 s. `LEAD` is what holds it there, and its value is measured
rather than chosen — the sweep is in the module, and 0.08 is the knee: at zero
the error is barely smaller and more than half of all clicks clip.

**Nothing stored is touched**, which is the point. The critical invariant
forbids stretching or offsetting stored word timestamps, and `speakers.rs`
assigns speakers by them. This is a reading applied where they become something
to click; the first word of every group keeps its measured value exactly, and a
group with no room — the next measured time not after this one — keeps the old
behaviour for all of it, because there is no honest estimate to make.

seconds already carried half a shift of slack that the frame count threw away.

the requirement is written as what the model accepts rather than as a time.

| processor | 1333.8 ms |

### 2026-08-13 — What the window has to read before it draws anything

The build has been warning about a 583 kB chunk for as long as there has been a
build, and the warning was right in an unusual way: this is a desktop
application, so nothing is downloaded and the size is not a transfer cost. It is
parse and execute, before the first paint, every start.

The archive is the only screen a start ever shows. Three screens that a start
never shows were in that chunk with it — the transcript, the settings and the
wizard — and they are now fetched separately:

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| read before the first paint | 583.34 kB | **457.08 kB** |
| transcript | — | 68.19 kB |
| settings | — | 44.58 kB |
| wizard | — | 16.52 kB |

### 2026-08-13 — Coming back to a transcript that is still playing

Asked for: returning to the page while audio is running should land on the last
highlighted word rather than at the top.

**It already did, and the reason it did is worth writing down**, because the
first reading of the code said it could not. The follow-along effect looks the
block up by element id, and after [the progressive
drawing](2026-08-13.md) the block being read is usually past the first forty and
has no element yet, so on arrival it leaves without doing anything. What was
missed is that the audio keeps running: a few seconds later the reading crosses
into the next block, `active?.id` changes, the effect runs again, and by then
the whole list is drawn. So it landed — one block late. The owner tried it and
said so before this was finished, which is the only reason it is described
correctly here.

Two things follow from that, and neither is the bug that was expected.

**It now waits on the drawn count as well.** `drawnBlocks` is in the dependency
list, so the effect looks again the moment the rest of the transcript appears
rather than at the next block boundary. On a recording whose blocks run long
that is the difference between immediate and a wait nobody can explain.

**Arriving glides, and that was the owner's call rather than a deduction.**
The first attempt made it jump: gliding from the top of a forty-five minute
transcript down to the middle is a long animation, and jumping seemed the
calmer choice. He tried it — *"now it jumps straight there, but it is missing
the smooth settling"* — and gliding stayed.

That answer made the code smaller rather than larger. A separate arrival branch
was only needed because arriving behaved differently; once it glides like
everything else the ordinary path already does the whole job, because on
arrival the list is at the top and the block is by definition out of sight.
Both refs the first attempt introduced are gone, and what is left of this
change is one item in a dependency list.

**The note in `progressiveList.ts` was wrong the moment this landed** and has
been corrected rather than left. It said nothing scrolls on arrival, which was
what made cutting the list short safe; one thing does now, and it says which,
and it says that anything else which jumps to a block on opening has to be told
about the drawn count too.

### 2026-08-13 — The one component the wizard calls required, from a host that deletes it

Reported an hour after 1.1.0 went out, from a deliberately wiped machine being
set up for a demonstration video: *Server odmítl soubor vydat* on the audio
component, and then, on a second press, a red *Stahování selhalo — Stahování už
běží* with no progress behind it.

Two defects, and the first one broke every fresh installation of 1.1.0.

**The archive was gone from the host, not stale in the catalogue.**
`components.json` pinned
`gyan.dev/ffmpeg/builds/packages/ffmpeg-9.0-essentials_build.zip`, which is a
404 today; gyan.dev now serves 9.0.1 and **deletes a numbered archive when the
next version replaces it**. So this was not a pin going out of date. It was the
file going missing, on a schedule nobody controls, for the one component the
wizard marks required — *Bez něj to nepoběží*.

The tracker's own comment shows the reasoning was sound and the host was not:
the numbered archive was chosen deliberately over the rolling `-release-` name,
"which means a different build every few weeks". Right idea, wrong shelf.

**The fix is the same builder on a shelf that keeps things.** Gyan publishes the
identical builds as release assets on `GyanD/codexffmpeg`. The proof they are
identical is in the catalogue: the digest GitHub reports for 9.0 is
`e6b54767…`, which is the digest that was already pinned here. And 9.0 is still
downloadable there after gyan.dev stopped serving it — the exact file whose
disappearance caused this.

So nothing about the security model moves. Same author, so `NOTICE` and *fetched
directly from its author* still hold; a fixed address for one specific file, so
SECURITY.md's *žádné „nejnovější"* still holds; a digest read from the publisher,
now GitHub's rather than gyan's `.sha256`. `fromGyan` is gone and ffmpeg tracks
like everything else. Also corrected on the way past: the wizard was totalling
ffmpeg as 85 MB, and it is 106.

**The second defect is what it said while doing it.** One download runs at a
time and the backend refuses the second call. That refusal was arriving on the
setup screen as a red *Stahování selhalo* — over a download that was running
perfectly well — and it called `setRunning(false)`, which took the progress view
down with it. So the answer to *is anything happening?* was a frightening no
while the truth was yes. The screen now stays where it is and watches: the
download already going emits the same events to the same listener, so there is
nothing to say and nothing to start.

### 2026-08-13 — Volocal 1.1.1, an hour and a half after 1.1.0

A patch, and the shortest gap between two releases this project has had. 1.1.0
shipped with a pin to an archive gyan.dev had deleted the day before, so every
fresh installation of it stopped at the one component the wizard calls required.

**The mirror was considered and declined.** Hosting the archive as an asset on
this repository's own releases was the obvious-looking answer to *so the user
always has a build to hand*, and it buys almost nothing: `GyanD/codexffmpeg` is
already a permanent GitHub release asset, exactly as permanent as ours would be.
The risk it would remove is *gyan deletes his GitHub repository*, which is far
smaller than the one already removed — his website rotating files, which is
policy rather than accident and is what happened.

What it would cost is real. `NOTICE` rests the licence position on the installer
not containing FFmpeg and it being fetched directly from its author; an archive
in our own release is us conveying a GPL v3 binary, with the obligation to
supply the corresponding source — ffmpeg's, every GPL library in that
configuration, and the scripts that built it. §6(d) allows pointing at somebody
else's server, but only while ensuring it stays available, which is not
something one can ensure about a server one does not run. Declined on
13 August; the owner's words were to leave it with gyan on GitHub.

**What the weekly job can and cannot do.** `update-components.yml` runs Monday
at six, and it has never run — it was added on Monday 10 August, after that
week's slot. Even if it had, it could not have caught this: on 10 August the
current version was 9.0 and the pin said 9.0, so there was nothing to change;
9.0.1 landed on Wednesday the 12th and deleted 9.0 in the same moment. And more
fundamentally, the catalogue is compiled into the binary, so a job that ran
every hour would keep this repository fresh while every installed copy kept the
address it was built with. Freshness is not the same property as reachability.

**Still owed, and agreed in that order**: a run-time fallback through `track`,
so that a copy already installed can recover on its own when a pinned address
stops answering, and the setup screen offering `bin_directory` at the moment a
required component fails rather than only in Settings.

### 2026-08-13 — Three seconds that were the model, not the code

Reported from the screen with a picture: the audio was beginning *Tak jo, tak
děkuji* while the block after it, *Možná se budete cítit*, was already
highlighted. Roughly three seconds of the transcript running ahead of the sound.

**This is the shape of the failure `CLAUDE.md` opens with**, and the temptation
it warns about is to reach for the stored timestamps. So nothing was touched
until it had been measured.

**Playback was ruled out first.** The recording is an MP3 and had its M4A
playback proxy; source and proxy report the same duration to four decimal
places — 2704.297708 s — so the proxy shifts nothing. And the discrepancy is not
a seek error in any case, which the next measurement settles.

**Then the audio was asked directly**, the way the original reproduction was
settled: thirty-five seconds around the spot cut from the source and transcribed
again with `large-v3`, independently of the archive.

| | the audio says | the archive said |
|---|---|---|
| *Tak konečně ses dostala…* | 4:42.58 | 4:42.580 |
| ***Tak jo, tak děkuji…*** | **4:49.84** | **4:46.50** |
| *Každý majitel…* | 4:57.94 | 4:57.90 |

The blocks on either side are right to within four hundredths. The one between
them is 3.34 s early — it sits in a gap where nobody is speaking. Nothing
accumulates and nothing is stretched: one block landed in the wrong place.

**The cause is the model.** The archive from before the machine was wiped holds
the same passage at 4:50.400, half a second from the truth, and it was
transcribed with `large-v3`. Today's was `large-v3-turbo-q5_0` — the fast model
a fresh installation recommends, which the owner had picked for speed. Same
audio, same code, different alignment. Worth knowing because it undermines
precisely the thing improved earlier today: a word click can only be as accurate
as the timestamps under it.

**One real finding survives the diagnosis.** `snap_to_sound` exists to move a
block's start onto the sound, and it could not help here:

```rust
// Never past the word that follows
limit = limit.min(next - onset::FRAME_SECONDS);
```

The shift is capped by the *second word's* timestamp. When a whole block is
misplaced, that second word is misplaced with it, so the cap is computed from
the same wrong data the correction exists to repair and nothing may move. It can
rescue a first word inside a block; it cannot rescue a block. That is true
whatever model is used, and it is written down here rather than fixed, because
fixing it means moving every word of a block and that is a decision to take
deliberately rather than in the middle of somebody's demonstration video.

**Two things this entry gets wrong elsewhere and corrects here.** The
investigation was reported to the owner as *hold every merge until the draft is
published*, and the draft had already been published two minutes after it was
made; nobody had looked again. And the caution written first was that a future
reader might blame the MP3 seek — which the owner rightly pointed out is
weaker now that the proxy exists. The caution worth keeping is the method: when
the sound and the highlight disagree, transcribe a piece of the audio
independently and compare. It took two minutes and the answer was unambiguous.

  wrong.
- Verified: the durations, the independent transcription, and the old archive's
