# 2026-08-16

### 2026-08-16 — The cube leaves the interface and the name closes to its own middle

The mark until today was an isometric cube in three colours, with the drawn
wordmark beside it in the archive's header. It is gone from all three places it
stood — the header, the archive's empty state, the transcript header — and what
replaces it was already inside the wordmark.

**`olo` is the middle of `volocal`**, and the proof it was drawn that way is in
the file: the smile spans x 20.35 to 34.97 and is centred at **27.66**, which is
the centre of `olo` (12.45 to 42.86) to the hundredth. Centred under the whole
word it would sit at x 41. So the header does not swap one drawing for another;
it crops to what the drawing already contained.

#### What the header does

At start the full name stands for 2500 ms, then closes over 620 ms: `v` and
`cal` fade, the middle slides left by 12.45, the ™ comes to rest against the
second `o`, and the box closes from 82 to 36.49 units. Under the pointer it
opens again in 310 ms — quicker on purpose, because the closing is staged and
this is an answer to a hand.

- **The ™ sits at gap 0**, chosen at the screen. A first attempt carried over
  the 3.15 the ™ has in the full word, and the owner said it read too far — that
  number is the distance after a straight `l`, and after a round `o` it is
  wrong. It can go to nothing because the two never meet: the ™ occupies y 0 to
  2.56 and the `o` starts at 4.37, so at zero they line up on a right edge.
- **`:focus-visible` beside `:hover`.** This mark is a button — it returns to
  the archive — so a pointer-only affordance would leave every keyboard reader
  with a name they never see.
- **Once per start, not per arrival.** The archive is left and re-entered all
  day; a mark that re-introduced itself each time would be one joke told twenty
  times before lunch. A module-level flag, set when the closing begins rather
  than when the effect runs, so a mount torn down before anything moved does not
  spend the intro.
- **The header now carries one drawing on every screen**, where the cube was
  everywhere and the wordmark only in the archive. That rationing existed
  because two marks side by side were too much anywhere else; closing to `olo`
  with its ™ removed the pair and with it the reason.

#### What the archive's empty state does

The same mark, written rather than placed. The outline stands in the panel's own
line colour from the first frame and a pen fills it with the text colour: eye,
stem, eye, smile — the order a hand takes, with the smile last because it is the
point of the drawing. It begins 260 ms after the header has finished, never
alongside it: both are the same drawing and two of them moving at once ask for
one pair of eyes twice.

The pen is a white stroke inside a `mask`, so what advances is the reveal of the
real shapes rather than a stroke pretending to be them. Three details are the
difference between that reading as writing and reading as a smear, and each was
a visible fault before it was fixed:

- **A flat front, not a round one.** A round cap is a disc of half the stroke's
  width; on a ring whose centreline is 4.35 across, that disc reaches parts of
  the letter the pen has not got to, and black appears where the pen is not.
- **Every path is longer than the shape it fills.** The ellipses run 380 degrees
  rather than 360, because a pen that starts at the top and returns to the top
  from the right left a notch in each eye. The stem overshoots 0.5 at both ends;
  the smile runs 13 degrees past each tip, whose real ends are cut at an angle a
  flat front cannot reach. Overshoot is always safe — the letterform clips it.
  Falling short never is.
- **`stroke-dasharray: 100 200` with the rest at 101.** At `100`/`100` the
  resting state sits exactly on the boundary between the drawn and undrawn
  halves of the pattern, and the browser's rescaling of the real curve onto
  `pathLength`'s hundred units leaves a sliver at each stroke's start — on a
  six-unit pen, a visible black tick on the outline before anything has begun.

**Strokes overlap by 35 %** and their durations are proportional to their
lengths, so the pen holds one speed and the short stem does not take as long as
a whole eye. 35 rather than more: above 48 % — the ratio of the smile's length
to the second eye's — the smile finishes before the eye above it, which puts the
punchline before the setup. At 35 it lands 123 ms after, last of everything.

#### Where the numbers live

`src/brandArt.ts` holds the nine outlines as `d` strings copied from
`wordmark.svg` character for character, the four pen centrelines, and the
timing. `wordmark.svg` keeps them as a flat list, which is right for a file
somebody opens in an editor and wrong for a header that has to move three of
them and leave the rest.

The geometry reaches the stylesheet as custom properties from that module rather
than being written out again in CSS, so a redrawn letterform cannot leave the
two disagreeing. What no check can catch is the pen: those centrelines are
derived from the outlines and nothing on screen says when they have gone out of
step — the mark simply starts writing itself beside its own shape.

- Files: `src/brandArt.ts` and `src/Brand.tsx` (new), `src/App.tsx`,
  `src/Library.tsx`, `src/Detail.tsx`, `src/css/01-base.css`,
  `src/css/02-library.css`.
- `.logotype` and `.header-brand-name` are deleted. Their numbers — 24 px cube,
  14.8 px wordmark, 5.6 between — were measured off `src-tauri/nsis/header.bmp`
  so the header would sit the way the installer sits, and each described a pair
  that no longer exists.
- **One of those numbers came back within the hour, and deleting it was a
  mistake.** The old rule also dropped the wordmark 1.9 px, and the comment
  beside it said why: the ™ lifts the top of the drawing's box and the smile
  under the `o` drops its bottom, so a box centred in the bar does not put the
  letters where the eye expects them. That reasoning was never about the cube —
  the letters run y 0.08 to 16.52 in a 25 tall box whichever way you look at it —
  so it should have survived the cube. The owner saw it immediately: *logo nám
  opticky letí nahoru*. It is 2 px now, on his word, where the artwork gave 1.9.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` skips both animations to their finished state. Not
  faster: absent.
- Verified: `npx tsc --noEmit`; `npm run build`, including `i18n:check` with the
  same 17 untranslated keys and one inconsistency as before, and `docs:check`
  clean; `npm test` 132.
- Not verified by machine: the intro must play once per launch and not again on
  returning to the archive from a transcript or from Settings; the pen must
  leave no outline showing at the end and no black on it at the start; hover and
  keyboard focus must both open the name; and the compact drop zone reached by
  scrolling must not restart the writing.

#### The application icon is the same crop again

The owner drew it: `olo` and its smile, black on a white square, 320 × 320 with
the drawing centred on 160 in both directions to the hundredth. So the mark on
the task bar, the mark in the header and the mark in the empty state are now one
drawing at three sizes, which is the whole argument of this change arriving
where it is seen most often.

`src-tauri/icons/app-icon.svg` is that source, and `tauri icon` renders the set
from it — it takes SVG directly, so nothing is rasterised by hand and the sizes
cannot drift from each other. It emits nineteen files including Android
mipmaps, iOS sets and Windows Store tiles; **six are copied in**, the six this
application actually ships and the five `tauri.conf.json` names. The rest would
be a platform's worth of files for platforms this application does not build.

**The white square is gone**, on the owner's word after seeing both variants
rendered onto a dark band and a light one. Without it the mark is cleaner on
light — no box edge — and weak on dark, black on near-black. His own task bar is
on the light theme, which is why the version that suits him is also the one that
is worst elsewhere, and that is written down here rather than discovered later:
**on a dark task bar this icon is close to invisible.** The answer, when somebody
wants it, is not a switch but a drawing — the square in one of the publisher's
three colours would satisfy both — and nobody has made it.

The drawing was also cropped. It occupied 77 % of the source canvas across and
61 % down, so at the size a task bar actually asks for it was a small mark in a
large empty square. The viewBox is now `19.95 19.95 280.10 280.10`, the same
centre with the letters filling 88 % of the width.

#### The blurred icon was a missing size, not a soft drawing

> Ikona v taskbaru je rozmazaná.

The generated ICO carried 16, 24, 32, 48, 64 and 256 — the set `tauri icon`
produces and, on most machines, the right one. His screen is 3840 × 2160 shown
at 2560 × 1440: **150 % scaling**, at which Windows asks the task bar for a
**36 px** icon. There is no 36 in that list, so the shell resampled 48 down to 36
every time it drew it. Nothing about the artwork was soft; a resample was.

`tauri icon` cannot choose what goes inside an ICO, so `scripts/make-ico.ps1`
assembles it: 16, 20, 24, 32, 36, 40, 48, 64, 96, 128 as DIB entries and 256 as
an embedded PNG. DIB below 256 because NSIS has historically been particular
about PNG-compressed ICO entries and the installer reads this file; PNG at 256
because a raw DIB that size is a quarter of a megabyte for no gain. Asking the
file for 24, 32, 36 and 48 afterwards returns each of them exactly, with no
scaling.

**Regenerating icons with `tauri icon` alone will silently undo this.** The 36
disappears, the file still works everywhere, and the fault only shows on a
machine at 150 %. That is why the script is in the repository rather than in a
transcript.

- Files: `src-tauri/icons/app-icon.svg` and `scripts/make-ico.ps1` (new), and the
  six icon files they generate.

#### The prototype is in the repository, not in a chat

`docs/prototypes/volocal-olo.html` is the page every number above was chosen on:
both animations running in a mock window, with sliders for the hold, the
collapse, the hover, the ™ gap, the writing time, the stroke overlap and the
easing, and a live readout of how far apart the second eye and the smile land.

It is kept because the numbers in `brandArt.ts` and `Brand.tsx` were tuned by eye
and will be tuned by eye again, and re-deriving them without it means guessing.
Its defaults are the values now in the code; if either side changes, change both,
or the page starts lying about the application it describes.

#### What is open, and none of it is small

- **The window's title bar.** The plan puts the mark of Znackarna — red
  triangle, green circle, blue square — in the top-left of the window.
  `tauri.conf.json` has no `decorations: false`, so that strip is Windows' own
  and its icon is the application's `.ico` and nothing else. Putting a drawing
  there means building the title bar by hand: minimise, maximise, close, the
  drag region, Snap Layouts, double-click to maximise. That is window
  management, not branding, and it is a change of its own.
- **The installer's own art** is the printed lockup, cube and all — `header.bmp`
  at 300 × 114 and `sidebar.bmp` at 328 × 632, both twice the sizes NSIS names
  because a 150 % screen resamples the single-size ones exactly the way it
  resampled the icon. It is the last place the cube survives, and the owner is
  drawing its replacement.
- **The face has no states yet.** The mark is written once and then holds still,
  and the owner wants it to answer for what the application is doing —
  transcribing, editing, waiting. Deferred on his word rather than forgotten:
  the drawing is already a face, the writing already has a pen and an order, and
  a state would be a second thing that pen can do. Whatever that turns into
  belongs in `docs/prototypes/volocal-olo.html` first.
- **The installer.** `header.bmp` and `sidebar.bmp` are the printed lockup, cube
  and all.
- **`src/mark.svg` is imported by nothing** and is kept because it is still what
  the icon and the installer show. `wordmark.svg` is likewise unimported and is
  kept as the drawing `brandArt.ts` copies from.
- **The empty state's own words** were to change with it — `Co dnes přepíšeme?`
  in place of `Sem přetáhněte svou nahrávku`, and `Přetažení souboru zahájí
  přepis.` in place of `Přepis se spustí automaticky. Data neopustí počítač.`
  Neither is done here. The second drops *Data neopustí počítač*, which is the
  sentence this product is built on and `SECURITY.md` is written about, and that
  is a decision to take deliberately rather than as the side effect of a shorter
  line.

### 2026-08-16 — Ten seconds, a signature in Informace, and the mark leads home from both screens

Four asks in one sitting, and the first of them moved something else.

**The full name stands for ten seconds, not two and a half** — *je to moc
rychlé*. The name is the only place the application says what it is called, and
a reader who looked away for a moment had missed it.

Two things had to follow, and neither was asked for:

- **The countdown is measured from when the application started, not from when
  the header mounted.** The header unmounts on the way into a transcript and
  mounts again on the way back, so at two and a half seconds a restart hardly
  mattered. At ten it would mean anybody moving around the application resets
  the clock every time and is introduced to the name over and over.
- **The written face stopped waiting for the header.** It used to begin 260 ms
  after the closing finished, on the argument that two of the same drawing
  moving at once ask for one pair of eyes twice. That argument was about two
  things *moving*, and during the hold the header does not move — it stands. Kept
  as it was, the empty state would have sat as a grey outline for eleven seconds,
  which reads as a screen that failed to load rather than as a mark waiting its
  turn. It writes 400 ms in now, and the header closes long after it.

**The window title is `volocal™`, lower case.** The strip directly above the
header said `Volocal` while the drawing under it has always been lower case.
`productName` stays capitalised: it names the executable, the install directory
and the path the updater looks in, and changing it would strand every machine
that already has the application.

**The publisher's mark is on the `Autor` row in `Informace`**, where the answer
to *who made this* already lived — in type, behind a generic glyph from the icon
set, while the publisher has a drawing of its own. It is the same three colours
the product's cube was built from before it left the interface, which is the
whole point: the palette did not go, it changed owner. The name stays beside the
mark, because a mark alone identifies the publisher to people who already know
them and this row exists for everybody else.

It is not in the header, which is where the question started. Three reasons:
this change spent the day taking a second mark *out* of the header; three
colours beside a black wordmark would leave the publisher outshining the product
in the product's own window; and the title bar was the right instinct precisely
because it is the operating system's chrome rather than the product's space.

**The mark leads to the archive from the transcript too.** In the archive's
header it always did — it is a button. In a transcript it was a decorative
`span` and the back button beside it carried the whole job, so the same drawing
was clickable on one screen and inert on the next. It is a button there now,
which also means it stops being `aria-hidden` and says where it goes.

- Files: `src/Brand.tsx`, `src/Detail.tsx`, `src/Library.tsx`,
  `src/Settings.tsx`, `src/css/05-later.css`, `src-tauri/tauri.conf.json`,
  `docs/prototypes/volocal-olo.html`.
- The publisher's name is one constant read twice — once by the drawing as its
  accessible name, once by the type beside it. `i18n:check` caught the first
  attempt, which had it written out twice: two literals are two chances to
  correct only one of them.
- The prototype's defaults follow the code, including the hold's slider now
  reaching fifteen seconds and the face's start no longer being expressed as an
  offset from the header.
- Verified: `npx tsc --noEmit`; `npm run build` with `i18n:check` and
  `docs:check` clean; `npm test` 132.
- Not verified by machine: the name must still be open ten seconds after launch,
  must close once and not again when returning from a transcript, and the mark in
  a transcript header must go back to the archive by pointer and by keyboard.

### 2026-08-16 — The installer loses the cube, and the last of it with it

The owner drew both bitmaps. `header.bmp` carries the wordmark; `sidebar.bmp`
stacks the written face large, the wordmark under it, and the publisher's mark
and name at the foot — product above, publisher below, the same pairing the
`Autor` row now shows.

Both keep 300 × 114 and 328 × 632 at 32 bpp, which are twice the sizes NSIS
names. That doubling is the same fault the icon had: a 150 % screen resamples a
single-size bitmap and the result is soft.

**With this the cube is gone from everywhere.** It was the last place it
survived, and the open item that said so is closed.

- Files: `src-tauri/nsis/header.bmp`, `src-tauri/nsis/sidebar.bmp`.
- `header_old.bmp` and `sidebar_old.bmp` were saved beside them and are not
  committed. The versions they back up are in this repository's history, which
  is the copy that does not rot.

### 2026-08-16 — The icon was thin, not blurry, and the cube had nineteen sizes

Two reports about the same file — *ikona v taskbaru je rozmazaná*, then *s
kostičkou to fungovalo OK* — and the second one was the useful half. The first
diagnosis stopped one step short.

**What was actually measured.** The generated ICO carried the six sizes
`tauri icon` produces. His screen is 3840 × 2160 at 150 %, so the task bar asks
for 36 px and the title bar for 24; the 36 was missing and the shell resampled
48 down to it. That was real and is fixed. But the cube's own ICO, recovered
from the commit before this branch, carried **nineteen** — 16, 20, 24, 28, 30,
32, 36, 40, 44, 48, 56, 60, 64, 72, 80, 96, 128, 192, 256. Replacing it with six
was a regression nobody had written down, and eleven was still fewer than what
had been there. `scripts/make-ico.ps1` builds all nineteen now.

**Sizes were not why the cube looked better.** Rendering both at 36 px beside
each other settles it: the cube is three solid faces filling the square, and the
`olo` is a thin black outline occupying two thirds of the height with nothing
behind it. On a task bar full of solid coloured marks, that reads as faded no
matter how many sizes the file holds.

That also corrects something claimed earlier the same day. The entry above says
the counters in the `o` mush together at small sizes and calls it a limit of the
drawing. On a plate they do not — white on blue has far more contrast than black
on nothing, and the same counters hold at 24 px. **Fineness was the smaller
problem; the absent mass was the real one.**

#### No plate, and how far that can be taken

The owner had asked for the plate to go and, shown four plated options against
it, said so again: *ideálně chci bez podkladu*. That is the decision, and it
costs presence — a plated mark still holds the task bar better and that is
written here rather than argued away.

What a bare mark can gain is size and weight, so it was given both. The drawing
fills 96 % of the canvas rather than 88, and the outlines carry a 9 unit stroke
in their own colour, which thickens every stem without redrawing anything. 16
was tried and rejected at the screen: it closes the counters and turns the face
into a blob, which is the fault the plate had just disproved.

- Files: `src-tauri/icons/app-icon.svg`, `scripts/make-ico.ps1`, and the six
  icon files they generate.
- Verified by asking the file: 16, 24, 36 and 48 each come back at their own
  size, with no scaling. The icon embedded in the running executable was checked
  too, because a stale build would have looked exactly like a bad drawing.
- Not verified by machine: how it sits among the other icons on a real task bar,
  which is the only place this was ever wrong.

### 2026-08-16 — Volocal 1.2.0

Five commits since `v1.1.1`, and two of them are whole rebuilds — Settings and
the first run, and the language model's context — so the number is a minor
rather than a patch. Nothing in it is a breaking change; everything in it is
something a reader will notice.

The version is set by `scripts/version.ps1`, which moves it in eight files: the
three that decide what version this is, and five that only say so to a reader.
That second group exists because on 12 August they all still said 0.9.0 with ten
versions released. A wrong number there breaks nothing and is read by everybody.

`CHANGELOG.md` is written in four parts rather than as a list of commits — the
interface, the language model, the mark, and what was fixed — because the
release covers two rebuilds and a rebrand, and a flat list would bury all three.

- Files: the eight `version.ps1` touches, and `CHANGELOG.md`.
- The two note texts are written separately from this: Czech for the dialog an
  installed copy shows before anybody agrees to a download, English for the
  release page. `release.ps1` documents why they are two texts rather than one
  translated — the audiences have never wanted the same thing.
- Not verified by machine, and it is the last thing before publishing: the
  installer has to install over an existing 1.1.1 and start, and the update has
  to be offered to a copy of 1.1.1 and accepted by it. A release that only
  builds is not a release.
