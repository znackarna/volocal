# 2026-08-16

### 2026-08-16 — The cube leaves the interface and the name closes to its own middle

The mark until today was an isometric cube in three colours, with the drawn
wordmark beside it in the archive's header. It is gone from all three places it
stood — the header, the archive's empty state, the transcript header — and what
replaces it was already inside the wordmark.

**`olo` is the middle of `volocal`**, and the proof it was drawn that way is in
the file: the smile spans x 20.35 to 34.97 and is centred at **27.66**, which is
the centre of `olo` (12.45 to 42.86) to the hundredth. Centred under the whole
word it would sit at x 41. So the header does not swap one drawing for another;
it crops to what the drawing already contained.

#### What the header does

At start the full name stands for 2500 ms, then closes over 620 ms: `v` and
`cal` fade, the middle slides left by 12.45, the ™ comes to rest against the
second `o`, and the box closes from 82 to 36.49 units. Under the pointer it
opens again in 310 ms — quicker on purpose, because the closing is staged and
this is an answer to a hand.

- **The ™ sits at gap 0**, chosen at the screen. A first attempt carried over
  the 3.15 the ™ has in the full word, and the owner said it read too far — that
  number is the distance after a straight `l`, and after a round `o` it is
  wrong. It can go to nothing because the two never meet: the ™ occupies y 0 to
  2.56 and the `o` starts at 4.37, so at zero they line up on a right edge.
- **`:focus-visible` beside `:hover`.** This mark is a button — it returns to
  the archive — so a pointer-only affordance would leave every keyboard reader
  with a name they never see.
- **Once per start, not per arrival.** The archive is left and re-entered all
  day; a mark that re-introduced itself each time would be one joke told twenty
  times before lunch. A module-level flag, set when the closing begins rather
  than when the effect runs, so a mount torn down before anything moved does not
  spend the intro.
- **The header now carries one drawing on every screen**, where the cube was
  everywhere and the wordmark only in the archive. That rationing existed
  because two marks side by side were too much anywhere else; closing to `olo`
  with its ™ removed the pair and with it the reason.

#### What the archive's empty state does

The same mark, written rather than placed. The outline stands in the panel's own
line colour from the first frame and a pen fills it with the text colour: eye,
stem, eye, smile — the order a hand takes, with the smile last because it is the
point of the drawing. It begins 260 ms after the header has finished, never
alongside it: both are the same drawing and two of them moving at once ask for
one pair of eyes twice.

The pen is a white stroke inside a `mask`, so what advances is the reveal of the
real shapes rather than a stroke pretending to be them. Three details are the
difference between that reading as writing and reading as a smear, and each was
a visible fault before it was fixed:

- **A flat front, not a round one.** A round cap is a disc of half the stroke's
  width; on a ring whose centreline is 4.35 across, that disc reaches parts of
  the letter the pen has not got to, and black appears where the pen is not.
- **Every path is longer than the shape it fills.** The ellipses run 380 degrees
  rather than 360, because a pen that starts at the top and returns to the top
  from the right left a notch in each eye. The stem overshoots 0.5 at both ends;
  the smile runs 13 degrees past each tip, whose real ends are cut at an angle a
  flat front cannot reach. Overshoot is always safe — the letterform clips it.
  Falling short never is.
- **`stroke-dasharray: 100 200` with the rest at 101.** At `100`/`100` the
  resting state sits exactly on the boundary between the drawn and undrawn
  halves of the pattern, and the browser's rescaling of the real curve onto
  `pathLength`'s hundred units leaves a sliver at each stroke's start — on a
  six-unit pen, a visible black tick on the outline before anything has begun.

**Strokes overlap by 35 %** and their durations are proportional to their
lengths, so the pen holds one speed and the short stem does not take as long as
a whole eye. 35 rather than more: above 48 % — the ratio of the smile's length
to the second eye's — the smile finishes before the eye above it, which puts the
punchline before the setup. At 35 it lands 123 ms after, last of everything.

#### Where the numbers live

`src/brandArt.ts` holds the nine outlines as `d` strings copied from
`wordmark.svg` character for character, the four pen centrelines, and the
timing. `wordmark.svg` keeps them as a flat list, which is right for a file
somebody opens in an editor and wrong for a header that has to move three of
them and leave the rest.

The geometry reaches the stylesheet as custom properties from that module rather
than being written out again in CSS, so a redrawn letterform cannot leave the
two disagreeing. What no check can catch is the pen: those centrelines are
derived from the outlines and nothing on screen says when they have gone out of
step — the mark simply starts writing itself beside its own shape.

#### The application icon is the same crop again

The owner drew it: `olo` and its smile, black on a white square, 320 × 320 with
the drawing centred on 160 in both directions to the hundredth. So the mark on
the task bar, the mark in the header and the mark in the empty state are now one
drawing at three sizes, which is the whole argument of this change arriving
where it is seen most often.

`src-tauri/icons/app-icon.svg` is that source, and `tauri icon` renders the set
from it — it takes SVG directly, so nothing is rasterised by hand and the sizes
cannot drift from each other. It emits nineteen files including Android
mipmaps, iOS sets and Windows Store tiles; **six are copied in**, the six this
application actually ships and the five `tauri.conf.json` names. The rest would
be a platform's worth of files for platforms this application does not build.

**The white square is gone**, on the owner's word after seeing both variants
rendered onto a dark band and a light one. Without it the mark is cleaner on
light — no box edge — and weak on dark, black on near-black. His own task bar is
on the light theme, which is why the version that suits him is also the one that
is worst elsewhere, and that is written down here rather than discovered later:
**on a dark task bar this icon is close to invisible.** The answer, when somebody
wants it, is not a switch but a drawing — the square in one of the publisher's
three colours would satisfy both — and nobody has made it.

The drawing was also cropped. It occupied 77 % of the source canvas across and
61 % down, so at the size a task bar actually asks for it was a small mark in a
large empty square. The viewBox is now `19.95 19.95 280.10 280.10`, the same
centre with the letters filling 88 % of the width.

#### The blurred icon was a missing size, not a soft drawing

> Ikona v taskbaru je rozmazaná.

The generated ICO carried 16, 24, 32, 48, 64 and 256 — the set `tauri icon`
produces and, on most machines, the right one. His screen is 3840 × 2160 shown
at 2560 × 1440: **150 % scaling**, at which Windows asks the task bar for a
**36 px** icon. There is no 36 in that list, so the shell resampled 48 down to 36
every time it drew it. Nothing about the artwork was soft; a resample was.

`tauri icon` cannot choose what goes inside an ICO, so `scripts/make-ico.ps1`
assembles it: 16, 20, 24, 32, 36, 40, 48, 64, 96, 128 as DIB entries and 256 as
an embedded PNG. DIB below 256 because NSIS has historically been particular
about PNG-compressed ICO entries and the installer reads this file; PNG at 256
because a raw DIB that size is a quarter of a megabyte for no gain. Asking the
file for 24, 32, 36 and 48 afterwards returns each of them exactly, with no
scaling.

**Regenerating icons with `tauri icon` alone will silently undo this.** The 36
disappears, the file still works everywhere, and the fault only shows on a
machine at 150 %. That is why the script is in the repository rather than in a
transcript.

- Files: `src-tauri/icons/app-icon.svg` and `scripts/make-ico.ps1` (new), and the
  six icon files they generate.

### 2026-08-16 — The icon was thin, not blurry, and the cube had nineteen sizes

Two reports about the same file — *ikona v taskbaru je rozmazaná*, then *s
kostičkou to fungovalo OK* — and the second one was the useful half. The first
diagnosis stopped one step short.

**What was actually measured.** The generated ICO carried the six sizes
`tauri icon` produces. His screen is 3840 × 2160 at 150 %, so the task bar asks
for 36 px and the title bar for 24; the 36 was missing and the shell resampled
48 down to it. That was real and is fixed. But the cube's own ICO, recovered
from the commit before this branch, carried **nineteen** — 16, 20, 24, 28, 30,

was a regression nobody had written down, and eleven was still fewer than what
had been there. `scripts/make-ico.ps1` builds all nineteen now.

**Sizes were not why the cube looked better.** Rendering both at 36 px beside
each other settles it: the cube is three solid faces filling the square, and the
`olo` is a thin black outline occupying two thirds of the height with nothing
behind it. On a task bar full of solid coloured marks, that reads as faded no
matter how many sizes the file holds.

That also corrects something claimed earlier the same day. The entry above says
the counters in the `o` mush together at small sizes and calls it a limit of the
drawing. On a plate they do not — white on blue has far more contrast than black
on nothing, and the same counters hold at 24 px. **Fineness was the smaller
problem; the absent mass was the real one.**

#### No plate, and how far that can be taken

The owner had asked for the plate to go and, shown four plated options against
it, said so again: *ideálně chci bez podkladu*. That is the decision, and it
costs presence — a plated mark still holds the task bar better and that is
written here rather than argued away.

What a bare mark can gain is size and weight, so it was given both. The drawing
fills 96 % of the canvas rather than 88, and the outlines carry a 9 unit stroke
in their own colour, which thickens every stem without redrawing anything. 16
was tried and rejected at the screen: it closes the counters and turns the face
into a blob, which is the fault the plate had just disproved.

### 2026-08-16 — The improved transcript stopped at a seventh, and the test agreed with the bug

> Když teď dělám AI vylepšený přepis, nepřepíše se celý dokument.

**Yesterday's context work is the cause, and the arithmetic is unambiguous.**
Sizing the window to the recording made the chunk a function of the context —
up to 56 320 characters — while the `max_tokens` every rewriting step carried
stayed the constant it had been when a chunk was 6000: **4096**, which is about
8192 characters of Czech. So the model was handed a whole document and allowed
to answer with roughly a seventh of it.

Nothing said so. `finish_reason` was never read, so an answer cut off at the
budget arrived through the same code path as one that had finished, and was
stored as a complete document.

#### The answer is sized from the question

A rewrite returns what it was handed: the improved transcript is the speaker's
own words with the punctuation repaired, a translation is the same content in
another language, a custom document is usually neither much shorter nor much
longer. So `max_tokens` is now 23/20 of the chunk — its own length plus 15 % for
the punctuation an edit adds and the paragraphs it opens — with a floor of 512
so a short chunk still has room to finish a sentence.

The window then has to hold both, which changes how the chunk is found. It was
a subtraction, `context - OUTPUT - OVERHEAD`, and a subtraction is precisely
what let the question grow while the answer's room stood still. It is a division
now: a window holds T and T·23/20, which is T·43/20, so the chunk is
`(context - overhead) × 20/43`.

At the ceiling that is 15 003 tokens in, 17 253 out, 512 of overhead — 32 768
exactly.

#### A truncated answer is refused, not kept

`finish_reason: length` now fails the step with `ai.answer_truncated`. A
document that stops mid-sentence is worse than no document: it looks finished,
it is exported and read as though it were, and the reader has no way to tell
which part of their recording is simply absent. With the answer sized from the
question this should be unreachable, which is exactly why it has to be loud if
it is ever reached again.

#### What this costs, and a correction

**The entry for 14 August claims an hour of speech goes to the model in one
request. It never could.** An hour in and an hour out is twice what a 32 768
window holds. What fits whole is about half an hour; an hour takes two requests.

That claim survived because the test that was meant to guarantee it measured
the question alone, and the test underneath it — *a chunk always leaves room for
the answer* — compared the chunk against `OUTPUT_TOKENS`, the same constant the
requests carried. Both agreed on 4096 and both were wrong together, so a chunk
of 56 320 characters looked as though it left room for its answer. The test asks
`answer_tokens` now, which is what a request actually sends, and a second test
states the cost outright rather than leaving it to be discovered.

- Files: `src-tauri/src/ai_edit.rs`, `src/locales/cs/errors.ts`,
  `src/locales/en/errors.ts`.
- Verified: `cargo fmt`; `cargo clippy --all-targets -D warnings` clean;
  `cargo test` 208, two of them the corrected and the new one;
  `npm run build`; `npm test` 132.
- Not verified by machine, and it is the whole point: a real recording, long
  enough to be cut in two, has to come back with its last sentence intact.

### 2026-08-16 — The stroke that was meant to add weight added blur instead

> Ta ikona se v taskbaru podivně rozlívá. […] Nene, spíš jde o to, že ty tahy
> jsou jakoby silnější a rozmazané.

The entry above says a bare mark was given what it could gain — 96 % of the
canvas instead of 88, and a 9 unit stroke in its own colour. **Both halves of
that were wrong, and the second was the harmful one.**

**The stroke thickens the ring outward and inward at once.** Adding 9 units to a
filled shape grows its outer edge by 4.5 and moves its inner edge in by the
same, so every counter in `olo` lost 9 units of width. At 36 px those counters
are about 2.5 px to begin with; taking a fifth off them is what turns them from
a hole into grey, and grey inside a fattened stroke reads exactly as *thicker
and smeared*. What was meant to add presence removed the one thing that made the
letterforms legible at that size.

**The width was an over-correction of a different complaint.** Told the mark
lacked weight beside its neighbours on the task bar, the answer reached for size
— and at 96 % the drawing ran to within 2 % of the canvas edge while every icon
around it sat inside its cell with room to spare.

So: **84 % and no stroke.** The letterforms are exactly the weight they were
drawn at, and the only change from the original artwork is that it fills a
little more of the square — 8 % of margin each side rather than 11.5.

**What this leaves unresolved is the original complaint**, and saying so is more
useful than a third attempt. A wide, unfilled, colourless mark in a square slot
will read as lighter than the solid coloured shapes beside it, and neither size
nor weight fixes that without breaking something else. The plate did fix it and
was declined. The remaining answer is a drawing rather than a parameter.

- Files: `src-tauri/icons/app-icon.svg` and the six it generates.
- Three attempts were judged by rendering the real pixels at 24 and 36 px and
  looking at them, which is how the second one was caught. The first two were
  judged by reasoning about what should help.

been written minutes earlier and still drew the cube. **Windows was serving both

**The cost of not knowing this was three rounds of design judgement on the wrong

- Nothing in the repository changed. This is written down because the next

### 2026-08-16 — The icon gets a plate after all, drawn rather than argued about

### 2026-08-16 — The plate is a squircle, and it had to come from the drawing rather than from an approximation

The plate arrived as `<rect rx="80">` and was installed as such. The owner then
said the shape was wrong — *já tam mám takové to apple ukosení hrany* — and he
was right about the shape while the file was not carrying it. **The corner
smoothing an editor applies to a rectangle is its own property, and SVG has no
primitive for it**, so exporting the rectangle flattens the curve back to a
plain radius. What reached this repository was a true rounded rectangle: a
straight edge meeting an arc, with the discontinuity that meets at.

Fitting one was tried and rejected, with numbers. A superellipse of order 5
approximated by cubics converged first-order rather than fourth — 3.86 px of
deviation at 256 px with sixteen segments, 1.93 with thirty-two, 0.96 with
sixty-four — so an invisible fit would have meant a path of tens of kilobytes.
**And the deeper objection is that it would have been a precise rendering of the
wrong curve**, because a superellipse is not what corner smoothing draws.

So the shape came from the drawing: the rectangle flattened in the editor, which
bakes the smoothing into Béziers that the export then carries. It is eight
cubics, 590 characters, and it is exactly what the owner drew rather than a
reading of it.

- Files: `src-tauri/icons/app-icon.svg` and the six it generates, rebuilt at all
  nineteen sizes.
- The whole file is verbatim apart from `width` and `height` on the `<svg>`
  element, raised from 320 to 1024 so the largest sizes rasterise from a big
  enough box. `viewBox` is untouched, so no geometry moves.
- The general lesson, which cost two rounds: **an exported vector says what the
  format can say, not what the editor was showing.** When a shape looks wrong
  after an export, suspect the export before suspecting either party's eye.

### 2026-08-16 — Windows never asked for the 36 px icon

> Kámo, všechny ostatní ikony jsou parádní a crisp a jen ta naše crap.

He was right, and the answer came from asking the shell rather than reasoning
about it. `SHGetImageList` reports what Windows actually holds for a file:

```
SHIL_LARGE       32 x 32
SHIL_SMALL       16 x 16
SHIL_EXTRALARGE  48 x 48
SHIL_JUMBO      256 x 256
```

**Four sizes, and they are not scaled by DPI.** A task bar at 150 % draws its
icons at 36 px, and 36 is not in that list — so the shell takes 48 and resamples
it down. The 36 px entry added this morning, and the nineteen-size ladder built
around it, are never consulted for the task bar at all.

That inverts what matters. It is not how the 36 px entry looks; it is **how the
48 px entry survives a 25 % reduction**. A bold shape with few thin features
survives it, which is why every neighbouring icon is crisp. Two counters and a
thin smile do not.

**Three rounds of icon work were spent on the wrong number.** The plate, the
stroke, the fill ratio — each was judged against a 36 px render that Windows
never draws. The one measurement that would have redirected all of it took a
single call.

- Nothing changes here yet; this is the fact the next attempt has to start from.
  Whatever is drawn must be judged as a 48 px bitmap reduced to 36, not as a
  36 px bitmap.
- The nineteen sizes stay. They are right for Explorer, for the title bar and
  for any host that does ask, and they cost nothing.

### 2026-08-16 — The title bar was stretching a 16 px icon to 24

> Jen ta ikonka v záhlaví okna je děsivá.

Measured rather than guessed, the same way the task bar was. `WM_GETICON` on the
running window answers:

```
ICON_SMALL   16 x 16
ICON_BIG     none
```

**A window's small icon is loaded at `SM_CXSMICON`, which is 16 in the units a
program asks in.** The title bar then draws it at what that comes to on this
screen — 24 px at 150 % — so Windows stretched a 16 px bitmap by half. That is
the whole of it, and none of it is the drawing's fault.

nineteen reach the executable — checked by enumerating `RT_ICON`, which returns
exactly nineteen — and 24 is among them. The window simply never asked for it.

than scaling one. **The group is resource 32512** — `IDI_APPLICATION`, found by
enumerating `RT_GROUP_ICON` rather than assumed.

## Obličej mrká, a širší úsměv se nekoná

Prototyp dostal sekci `Obličej mrká`: značka v klidu, která jednou za pár
sekund mrkne. Mrknutí je `scaleY(.08)` na obou `o`. Podstatné je
`transform-box: fill-box` — bez něj se `transform-origin: center` počítá
z viewBoxu, takže by se obě oči zmáčkly k jednomu bodu mezi sebou; s ním má
každé svůj vlastní střed a zavře se na místě. `.08` a ne nula, aby po zavřeném
oku zůstala skulina a ne plný puntík. Interval má rozptyl ±40 %, protože
pravidelné mrkání je metronom, ne obličej.

**Tišší verze mrkání byla zamítnuta.** Připomínka byla, že mají mrknout jen
prázdné plochy uvnitř `o`, ne celý obrys — *takhle je to docela okaté*. Jde to:
`o` je jedna cesta ze dvou podcest oddělených `ZM`, takže se dá rozdělit, obrys
nechat stát a díru z něj vyříznout maskou, kterou pak hýbe animace; díry tím
zůstanou průhledné, ne přebarvené na pozadí. Postavené to bylo a odpověď byla
*to předchozí mrknutí bylo lepší*. Vráceno na celé oko. Zůstává tu proto, že to
byla správná otázka a špatná odpověď, a příště ji nemá cenu klást znovu.

**Širší úsměv na hover se dvakrát nepovedl a je odložený.** První pokus ho
zvětšoval kolem středu jeho oblouku — to mění poloměr, a větší poloměr nečte
jako širší úsměv, ale jako obličej, který se přiblížil. Druhý pokus tu námitku
respektoval: úsměv přestal být výplní a stal se tahem po téže kružnici
(poloměr 8,812, střed 27,66 / 13,81, tloušťka 3,103, rovná čela), dvě poloviny
od spodku ven, `pathLength="100"` a `stroke-dashoffset` říká, kolik je vidět.
V klidu 44,9° na každou stranu, což je přesně to, co má původní kresba: konec
oblouku vychází na (21,440 / 20,052) proti (21,44 / 20,05) v kreslení.

Odpověď byla, že je tam divný artefakt. Odhad byl spára uprostřed — dvě rovná
čela na tomtéž bodě se rasterují každé zvlášť a antialiasing je nesloží do plné
černi — a poloviny dostaly třístupňový překryv. Artefakt zůstal.

Pak přišlo měření místo hádání: vyrenderovat obě varianty vedle sebe a podívat
se. `lap.svg` s překrytými tahy a `orig.svg` s původní výplní **vypadají
naprosto stejně** — táž tloušťka, táž šikmá čela, žádný šev. Tah tedy původní
kresbu reprodukuje přesně a artefakt v té geometrii není. Kde je, nevím, a to
je důvod, proč tu úsměv teď není: **třetí oprava naslepo by byl třetí odhad.**

  `src/Brand.tsx` mrkání ani úsměv zatím nemá.
- Ověřeno: skript stránky se parsuje, po `alive-smile` a `alive-pupil` v souboru

- Pozor na renderování lokálně: **resvg ignoruje `pathLength`**, prohlížeče ne.

### 2026-08-16 — The smile works on the third attempt, and the face goes into the application

The entry above closes with *třetí oprava naslepo by byl třetí odhad*. It was
right to stop, and it was also stopping one step short of the answer.

**The third attempt did not fix the stroke. It removed it.** The smile is a
filled path in the drawing, and both failed attempts had turned it into
something else — first a scaled copy, then a stroke along its own centreline
with `pathLength` and a dash pattern deciding how much showed. It is a filled
path again here: an annulus sector, two concentric arcs closed by radial cuts,
regenerated for whatever angle is wanted. Opening it changes one number.

That is what makes it worth writing down rather than just shipping. **Nobody
ever found the artefact.** What went instead was every mechanism it could have
been in — two pieces meeting in the middle, their flat ends, the browser's
rescaling of a real curve onto `pathLength`'s hundred units. The bug was not
diagnosed; the surface it lived on was removed. When a defect resists two
careful fixes, taking away the machinery is a better move than a third guess at
which part of it is wrong.

#### The geometry was measured, and the old numbers were off

The failed attempt used radius 8.812 about (27.66, 13.81), resting at 44.9°.
Fitted properly to `GLYPH.smile`, the shape is:

| | |
|---|---|
| centre | 27.66 / 13.8330 |
| radii | 7.2400 inner, 10.3428 outer — 3.1028 thick |
| rest | **exactly 45.000°** each side |

Every on-curve point of that path lies on one of those two circles to within
0.0001 of a unit, and the generated path at 45° returns the drawing's own four
corners to four decimal places. So this is not a shape resembling the smile; it
is the smile, expressed as something that can open.

**`src/brandArt.test.ts` asserts all of it.** That file's header has always
warned that if the letterforms are redrawn, nothing on screen says the derived
paths have gone out of step — the mark simply starts writing itself beside its
own outline. The pen still has no such check. The smile now does.

#### What the face does when it is idle

- **It blinks.** The whole `o` squashed to `scaleY(.08)`, not the counter
  inside it — the quieter version was built and rejected. `transform-box:
  fill-box` is what makes it work at all: without it `transform-origin: center`
  is measured from the viewBox and both eyes squash toward one point between
  them instead of closing where they stand. `.08` and not zero, or a closed eye
  is a solid dot rather than a slit.
- **It smiles now and then**, opening from 45° to **55°** — chosen at the
  screen, where 62 was the prototype's default and read as too much.
- **It smiles under the pointer, and holds.** That is the difference from the
  unprompted one, which leaves by itself after 1.2 s: a mood ends on its own, an
  answer ends when the question does.
- **Intervals carry ±40 % of jitter.** A rhythm that can be predicted after
  twenty seconds is more distracting than the movement.

Three states it deliberately does **not** have:

- **It does not blink while the mill runs.** Turned on its side it is not a
  face, and a blink there is a roller squashing flat. This closes the open
  question the prototype recorded as *mrká i během přepisu?* — the answer is no.
- **It does not blink while it is being written.** The pen has not reached the
  eyes yet.
- **The header wordmark gets none of this.** Its hover already opens the full
  name, and a second thing answering the same gesture on the same element would
  be two answers to one question. At 14.8 px a blink would also be noise rather
  than life.

`mouseenter` rather than `pointerenter`: on a touch screen the pointer events
open the smile on a tap and `pointerleave` may never arrive, which leaves it
stuck open. Hover-only means the keyboard does not get it, and that is
acceptable **while this is decoration** — if the mark ever becomes a control in

#### Why the smile runs its own loop

A recomputed path cannot be handed to a CSS transition, so it steps on
`requestAnimationFrame` and writes the attribute directly, off React — it
changes every frame while opening, and re-rendering the mark sixty times a
second to move one attribute would be paying a lot for nothing. Out with a small
overshoot (~5 %), back without: a smile that arrives and stops dead is
mechanical, and coming back there is no reason for the face to shoot it inward.
