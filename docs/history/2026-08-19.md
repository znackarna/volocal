# 2026-08-19

### 2026-08-19 — The one preview becomes five, driven by hand

The page carried a single window under the hero. It now carries a carousel of
five, and the reader moves it: arrows, dots, and the arrow keys. Nothing
advances on its own, which is the whole decision — a window that changes while
somebody is reading it moves the thing they were looking at.

The five: the transcript as it stands after processing; the archive with one
recording being transcribed; the *AI nástroje* dialog over the transcript; the
finished document with its four tabs; and the first run asking its one question.

**The archive window had to be recovered rather than rebuilt.** It was deleted
from `index.html` earlier the same day, and `docs/site/` is not in git, so there
was no history to go back to. The copy that survived was the morning's published
artifact, saved to disk by a `WebFetch` earlier in the session; the window came
out of that file. Worth writing down as a fact about the current arrangement:
**an untracked folder has no undo.** Everything in `docs/site/` is one careless
deletion away from being gone, and the only reason today's was not is that a
tool had happened to keep a copy.

**Three things the dialogs needed**, and all three are the same class of fault —
a component built for a window, drawn inside a picture of one:

- `.dialog-overlay` is `position: fixed`, so inside a preview it would veil the
  page rather than the window. The page draws `.shot-dialog` instead, absolute
  within the shot.
- It was missing the blur. `src/css/04-settings.css` puts `blur(3px)` under the
  veil and says why: the scrim darkens the window but leaves every edge behind
  the dialog as sharp as the dialog's own, so two planes compete for one focus.
  Copied, at the same 3 px.
- A dialog sizes itself against the viewport, and here the viewport is the
  browser rather than the 744 px window, so it grew past the frame and its
  footer was clipped. `.shot-dialog .dialog` is capped at 86 % of the shot and
  the text inside scrolls, which is what the application does.

**The glow was being cut off.** The carousel has to clip the slides beside the
current one, and with no padding it clipped the current window's shadow too.
The viewport takes 26/48 px of padding and gives it straight back with a
negative margin, so the slides keep their width; and the slides stand 120 px
apart rather than edge to edge, so what shows in those 48 px is ground and not
the next window. `preview.js` carries that gap as `SLIDE_GAP`, because the track
now shifts by a slide *plus* one of them.

Files: `docs/site/index.html`, `docs/site/preview.js`.

Verified: rendered headless at 1280 in the light palette with the track forced
to each slide in turn, and read at each. The bundle is 621 kB against 530.


### 2026-08-19 — The archive view scrolls its own header

The second view opened with the hero already collapsed, which is a state the
reader has no reason to recognise. It now opens with the hero standing and rolls
up two seconds later — `collapsed` on `.archive-sticky` and `compact` on
`.archive-drop-zone`, the two classes the application itself sets once somebody
scrolls the list, on the transitions it already carries: the mark drops from
63 px to 38, the heading from 26 to 15.

**Once, not on a loop.** A header that reopened itself would be a window moving
with nobody touching it, which is the same objection that keeps the carousel
manual. Under `prefers-reduced-motion` it does not happen at all and the hero
simply stands.

The list under it carries the recording being transcribed, one finished
recording, and then the *Složky* block with one folder — which is the
application's own order: recordings first, drawers below them.


### 2026-08-19 — What the gains section went through, and what it cost

Five arrangements in one day, and the last one is the second one. Written down
because the middle three were each an improvement on paper and each worse on the
screen.

1. **Eight rows with icons**, two columns, a hairline over each. Where the day
   started.
2. **Three lead cards and ten in a masonry.** The hierarchy was the owner's ask
   and it holds: three cards say why somebody installs this at all, the rest are
   functions. Two new ones came in here — the watched folder and folders in the
   archive — and the icons are `LINE_ICONS`, never a ninth drawn by hand.
3. **An accordion**: the ten as full-width rows that open on hover. Wrong shape,
   and said so immediately — ten bars across 1180 px is a settings screen.
4. **Small blocks**: the same folding, in a four-column grid. Better, and still
   wrong for a reason the owner named exactly — *nedávají smysl, vypadá to jako
   každý jiný AI cringe*. He is right, and the fault is the genre: a drawing, a
   bold name and one sentence, repeated twelve times, is the shape every
   generated landing page has.
5. **A paragraph and a specification.** The experiential half became prose in
   the reading face, the factual half a dry term-and-line table. The form was
   right and it threw away the copy — *Nemusíte u toho být.* and *Poznámky, co
   drží slovo.* do not survive being summarised into a list of formats.

So the ten came back with **every sentence intact**, in the shape the page
already uses two sections down: two columns, a hairline above each row, the
application's icon, the gain in bold, one sentence. No boxes, no folding,
everything visible at once.

**The lesson, and it is the reusable part:** the fault was never the amount of
information, it was that a card is a container with no argument in it. What
makes the section not read as generated is the writing — *Vidíte, co slyšíte.*,
*Poznámky, co drží slovo.* — and every arrangement that hid, shortened or
summarised that writing made the page worse, however much better the layout got.
When the shape is the problem, change the shape; do not pay for it in copy.

Ordered by what decides a reader, and the order reads across rather than down,
which the masonry could not do: the gesture the product exists for, then owning
the text, then knowing where to look, then in and out, then the rest.


### 2026-08-19 — What it runs on, with the program's own numbers

A new section, at the owner's ask: Rust and Tauri, why it is quick, what
hardware, the choice of model, and where the computing happens.

Everything in it is checked against the repository rather than written from
memory:

- **Tauri 2, Rust core, React over the system WebView, SQLite** — `README.cs.md`.
  whisper.cpp for the transcription, Silero VAD for the speech.
- **Rychlý or Přesný**, changeable later, finished transcripts unaffected —
  `wizard.quality.*`.
- **CUDA or Vulkan, chosen by the machine**, Vulkan covering integrated
  graphics, and the processor build always on disk so the program runs without a
  card — `tools.rs` and `download.rs`, where the processor build is described as
  the floor precisely because a graphics build dies with its driver.
- **An hour of recording in about a minute** on a card and eight on a processor
  for the fast model; four and thirty-five for the accurate one. Those are
  `estimatedMinutes` in `SetupWizard.tsx`, which is the only measured claim about
  speed this repository holds.

**What is deliberately not on the page: recommended hardware in numbers.**
Nothing here has ever measured a memory threshold — the comment beside
`recommendedQuality` says so outright, that deciding by memory would need a
number nobody has established. So the section says models take 700 MB to 1.7 GB
and that Windows 10 or 11 is the requirement, and stops. If figures are ever
wanted there, they have to be measured first.


### 2026-08-19 — The day's copy, and one deletion that took a tag with it

The owner rewrote most of the page's text through the day. The lede settled at
*Volocal přepisuje mluvené slovo pomocí lokálních jazykových modelů. Rozpozná
řečníky. Opraví chyby. Zpracuje libovolný zvukový formát, video nebo online
stream. Zdarma. Bez ztráty kontroly a soukromí.* — and **Zdarma** is what
finally backs the headline, which had been telling readers to cancel a
subscription on a page that never said the program costs nothing.

The headline itself ended the day formal: **Zapněte počítač. Vypněte
předplatné.** The informal version was deliberate for as long as it carried the
*notes / noťas* joke; with that gone there was nothing left for the register to
buy, and the page now speaks one way from the title to the footer, as the
interface does. *Vypněte* over *Zrušte* on the argument that the pair is one
switch in two directions, and that a subscription cannot literally be switched
off is exactly what makes it read as an image rather than an instruction.

Also his: *Zpracování nepotřebuje internet* as the privacy heading, with the
telemetry sentence moved above the three exceptions rather than under them;
*Kdyby vás zajímalo, proč to jede jako z praku.*; three first-run steps rewritten
as statements and set as cards with icons; and the SmartScreen paragraph reduced
to plain text with no styled parts — three highlighted fragments in three lines
had made one instruction look like three kinds of type.

**The limits are hidden, not deleted.** *Co nedělá dobře* sits in an HTML comment
with a note saying it is bound for a FAQ. Its copy is the owner's, and it is
where that FAQ will start; the `.minor` rules are left in the stylesheet waiting
for it. Worth recording that this reverses 18 August, where setting the limits at
the same size as the gains was the point. The argument then was that a page
whispering its faults does the opposite of what the list is for. That still
holds — which is why the block is parked rather than dropped.

**And a correction owed from yesterday's clean-up.** Removing the three small

because a browser closes what an author does not, so nothing looked wrong and
nothing reported it; the markup was simply invalid from that point to the end of
the section. Found while adding a class to that same tag. It is back.

Dead rules went with the day's changes rather than being left: `.trio` and the
`.screen*` set with the three crops, `.key` and `ol.rows` with the numbered
steps, `.footnote` with the paragraph that moved into a lede.
