### 2026-09-03 — The title says what the program does, in his words

`<title>Volocal</title>` was seven characters and named only the brand, so
somebody searching for what the program does could not arrive here. The better
line existed already — `og:title` is *Zapněte počítač. Vypněte předplatné.* —
but that one is shown when a link is pasted, never when somebody is searching.

Three drafts were refused, and rightly: they were written for the search engine,
not for a reader — *Volocal — přepis nahrávek do textu ve vašem počítači* and
two more like it, category labels with a dash where this page speaks in
sentences. His own line replaced all of them:

> Volocal — přepis řeči bez cloudu a předplatného

47 characters, inside the 45–60 that survives uncut, and the name first because
whoever knows Volocal searches for it by name.

The description was 189 characters, and a result page cuts around 155 — so what
disappeared was *Bez ztráty kontroly a soukromí.*, the strongest sentence in it.
It was also a day out of date, still saying *rozpozná řečníky* and nothing about
languages. His replacement is 102 characters, so nothing is cut at all:

> Přepis řeči bez cloudu a předplatného. Běží ve vašem počítači, rozpozná
> jazyky, řečníky a opraví chyby.

He corrected it twice inside the minute, and both corrections are right. The
colon after *počítači* promised a definition and got a list, so it is a comma;
and *jazyk* became *jazyky*, because the singular claims only that the program
detects which language it is hearing, where the plural claims what shipped this
week — two of them in one recording, told apart.

*Bez cloudu* is the privacy claim said positively and said first, which is what
the old sentence could not manage from the far end of 189 characters.

English follows: *Volocal — speech to text without the cloud or a subscription*
(60) and *Speech to text without the cloud or a subscription. It runs on your
own computer: it spots the language, marks the speakers and fixes mistakes.*

**And a fault the English page turned up on the way.** `twitter:title` and
`twitter:description` were left out of the list in `translateTag` that decides
which `content` attributes are a machine quoting the page to a person — so
`/en/` had been serving a Czech Twitter card for as long as those tags have
existed. `og:` was in the list and `twitter:` was not, which is why nobody saw
it: the two carry the same words and only one of them was checked. Both are in
the list now, verified by reading the built English head rather than by
reasoning about the regex.

### 2026-09-03 — The description stops repeating the title

He noticed it in the English and it was true of both: the title said *přepis
řeči bez cloudu a předplatného* and the description opened with the same words.
In a search result those two lines sit one above the other, so half the
description was spent saying again what the reader had just read.

His replacement says what the title does not:

> Rozpozná jazyky, řečníky a opraví chyby. Zvuk, video i online stream.
> Zdarma pro Windows 10 a 11.

96 characters, so nothing is cut, and it took three passes to get there because
the first two were still repeating the title. He caught it himself: *bez cloudu
je jasné, že to je lokálně, ne?* It was doubled twice over — the title's *bez
cloudu* and the description's *běží pouze ve vašem počítači* are one claim, and
so are *bez předplatného* and *zdarma*. What is left is only what the title does
not say: two languages in one recording, the speakers, the corrections, and that
it takes video and streams as well as sound.

The English drops the subject the same way, verb first, which is the register
the rest of the page's short claims are already in: *Spots the languages, marks
the speakers and fixes mistakes. Audio, video and online streams. Free for
Windows 10 and 11.*

**What this costs, recorded so the decision is not re-made by accident.** In a
search result the title sits directly above the description, so the repetition
was pure waste. In a pasted link it does not: there the line above is *Zapněte
počítač. Vypněte předplatné.*, which says nothing about the cloud — so the card
in Slack or on LinkedIn no longer mentions privacy at all. The two could be
split, `description` for search and `og:description` for the card, at the price
of one more text to keep in step. Not done, and his to reverse.

English follows: *Volocal runs only on your own computer. It spots the
languages, marks the speakers and fixes mistakes. Free, for Windows 10 and 11.*

### 2026-09-03 — A card for a pasted link

Every link to volocal.app in Slack, on LinkedIn, in a message has been a bare
text card. The head used to carry a paragraph arguing that this was right —
that no picture of this product is anything but a screenshot, and a bad preview
card is worse than none. Jakub reversed it, on the ground that the argument does
not cover a brand card, and the paragraph itself went yesterday with the
comments.

**The artwork is his.** Two of them arrived, light and dark, at 2400 × 1260: the
claim in Geist over most of the field, the three shapes of the mark reduced to a
triangle, a circle and a square in the top corner, the wordmark bottom left and
the address bottom right. The cube is deliberately not on it — *tu kostku tam
nedávejme vůbec* — and the flat shapes say the same thing without competing with
the sentence, which the cube did at that size.

The dark one is on the page. In Slack and on LinkedIn the stream of cards is
white, so a dark field is the only one of the two that stops the eye; the light
one is the safer card and disappears into the feed. Downscaled to 1200 × 630
with Lanczos, 96 KB.

Four things had to be true for it to work, and each was checked rather than
assumed:

- **`twitter:card` from `summary` to `summary_large_image`.** Left as it was,
  the card stays a thumbnail and the image is wasted.
- **Absolute URLs.** `https://volocal.app/og.png` rather than `og.png`, because
  the English page sits one directory deeper and a relative path would resolve
  to `/en/og.png`, which does not exist.
- **The copy list in `site.yml`.** A file that is not on that list never
  reaches `_site`, and the card would point at a 404 — which is worse than the
  bare card it replaces.
- **`og:image:alt` needs its English.** It is a sentence said to a person, so it
  goes through the dictionary like the rest: `og:image:alt` joins the list in
  `translateTag`, and the English head was read back to prove it swapped.

Files: `docs/site/index.html`, `docs/site/og.png`, `docs/site/en.json`,
`docs/site/translate.mjs`, `.github/workflows/site.yml`.

### 2026-09-03 — The English claim finds a rhyme the Czech cannot have

The hero was *Computer on. Subscription off.*, a faithful English of *Zapněte
počítač. Vypněte předplatné.* It is now:

> Transcription on. Subscription off.

*Transcription* and *subscription* end on the same syllable, which is a gift
English has here and Czech does not — no Czech pair of *přepis* and
*předplatné* rhymes. This is the rule about both halves being written as native
copy rather than as a translation, doing what it was written for: the two
languages now say the same thing with different means.

**The Czech stays as it is**, and the reason is the one that decided against
*Zapněte přepis*: the sentence lives on a contrast, and *počítač* is what
replaces the subscription — your own machine instead of somebody's server. A
transcript is what you get from the cloud too, so a Czech line with *přepis* in
both halves argues with nothing. English can afford the noun because the rhyme
carries the opposition instead.

Also weighed and refused: *Transcribe. Unsubscribe.*, which is the better line
of the two — two verbs, two acts, a tighter rhyme on the stressed syllable — and
*Nastartujte přepis. Zrušte předplatné.*, which loses the on/off pair the whole
construction rests on.

Three keys moved together: the hero's first sentence, `og:title` and
`twitter:title`, all of which carry it. The card image is Czech and is
untouched — but worth remembering that the sentence is baked into `og.png`, so
a change to the Czech would need the artwork redrawn.

Files: `docs/site/en.json`.

### 2026-09-03 — The English page gets its own card

The card carries the claim as artwork, so one file could only ever be right in
one language: an English reader was being shown a Czech sentence in the picture
and an English one under it. He asked which it should be, and the answer is the
page's own — a card exists to buy a click, and a sentence the reader cannot read
does not buy one.

So there are two, both his, both the dark of the pair: `og.png` with *Zapněte
počítač. Vypněte předplatné.* and `og-en.png` with *Transcription on.
Subscription off.* Each is 1200 × 630, downscaled from his 2400 × 1260 export.

The mechanism is one line: a `HEAD_SWAPS` pair rewriting the URL. It covers both
the `og:image` and the `twitter:image` that name the file, because a swap
replaces every occurrence rather than the first — and if the page ever stops
carrying that URL, `translate.mjs` throws instead of quietly serving the wrong
card. The `alt` needed no mechanism at all, being a sentence in the dictionary;
only its English changed, since the words it describes did.

Both files are on the copy list in `site.yml`. Read back from the built English
head rather than reasoned about.

Files: `docs/site/og-en.png`, `docs/site/translate.mjs`, `docs/site/en.json`,
`.github/workflows/site.yml`.

### 2026-09-03 — The page tells a search engine what it is

Nothing on it did. A crawler had to infer from prose that Volocal is a program,
that it runs on Windows and that it costs nothing. There is a block for saying
so outright, and he asked for it after being told what it is: text no reader
ever sees, addressed to machines.

It is a `SoftwareApplication`: name, address, language, category, `Windows 10,
Windows 11`, the card as its image, the releases page as where it is downloaded,
an offer at price 0 CZK, and značkárna s.r.o. as publisher. Every one of those
already stood in words on the page; none of them stood in a form a machine reads
without guessing.

**No version in it.** The version in the hero is rewritten at deploy time by
`site.yml`, which replaces `Verze …` and asserts it hit twice per page. A
`softwareVersion` here would either go stale on its own or make that count
wrong, and neither is worth what it would add.

**No sentences in it either**, which settles the trap this step came with:
`translate.mjs` passes the contents of a `<script>` through untouched — rightly,
since the rest of them are code — so a Czech sentence in that block would still
be Czech on `/en/`. Two values do belong to the page rather than to the program,
the address and the language, and they are named as `HEAD_SWAPS` pairs. The
image needed nothing: the swap that gives `/en/` its own card replaces every
occurrence of that URL, and the block's is one of them.

`bundle.py` needed telling. It strips the head and then checks that nothing was
left between the title and the first `<style>`; the block would have failed that
check. It removes it now and counts what it removed, in the same spirit as the
counting script — a bundle is one file handed to somebody, not the site, and has
no business describing the site to a search engine.

Read back from both built pages rather than reasoned about: Czech says
`volocal.app/` and `cs` and `og.png`, English says `volocal.app/en/` and `en`
and `og-en.png`.

Files: `docs/site/index.html`, `docs/site/translate.mjs`, `docs/site/bundle.py`.
